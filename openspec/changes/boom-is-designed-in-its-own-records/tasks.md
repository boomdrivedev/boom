strategy and no private path (`core.no-commercial-strategy`,
`core.private-sources-by-words`, design, *What stays out, and the word

`core.<name>`, never with the name in a code span; a number quoted as an
example, another repository's id among them, is in a code span (design,
*Names in this repository*). A ruling of the user's is cited by their
words and its date, never by a card's or a request's id. No file says
"keeper".

## Workflow follow-up

- Once the work is committed, `sluice exec main` runs the gate's checks here, as the brief asks; sluice runs the whole gate, `#check-fast` on `main`, when the change lands.
- The rules the records imply are drawn into `docs/architecture/core.md` in a separate step, as the user decided on 2026-11-01.
- flume's launch field and `Claim:` trailer, millpond's send filling it, and sluice's after-landing step are those tools' own changes (design, *Outside this change*).
- Each record stays proposed until the user has read it as a record and said yes; their yes is written one commit per record, in the order of design.md's section The records, each setting `status: accepted` and its `Status:` line to the date of their yes (design, *The records' form*). It is the user's act, not a task.
- The design's two questions for the user, where the four rules go and whether the family takes up names, not numbers, are theirs (design, *Open Questions*); the tasks follow the recommendations, and the other answer to the first moves the four rules' edits of `core.md` out of the records task.
- Where the user decides the family takes up names, not numbers, the requirements style in agentplot/blueprints and each tool's records, rules and check change by their own changes (design, *Outside this change*).
- The change is archived with `openspec archive` once it is verified, so `openspec/specs/` holds `record-identity`, `design-statement` and `design-pages`.
